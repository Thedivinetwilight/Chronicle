## 1. What changed, in one paragraph

Chronicle gained a visual identity that is its own rather than a palette swap on
a generic dashboard: a set of fourteen hand-authored line marks wired into
navigation and empty states, deterministic printed-plate covers for every
chapter, path, collection and trip, and a day plate in the Today masthead. The
shell was reorganised into three named groups so every destination has a home,
Today was rebuilt so the date is the headline and the writing box is the only
prominent thing on the page, and the Life Map gained a shape legend plus a
structural distinction between its two kinds of line. Roughly forty sentences of
repeated privacy boilerplate, filler and self-explanation were removed. AI was
removed from the interface entirely (§7).

---

## 2. Art direction

Constraints from the brief: bespoke, repo-native, small, consistent line weight,
responsive, decorative art marked decorative, meaningful art alt-texted, no emoji
as icons, no clip art, no mismatched icon families, no stock packs.

All artwork is hand-authored SVG in `src/ui/art/`, drawn on a grid with a fixed
stroke weight and no filters, gradients or embedded images. Nothing was
generated by an image model. **It is drawn in code by a person editing paths —
it is not literal ink on paper, and the report does not claim otherwise.**

### Marks — `Mark.tsx`

Nine marks are in use, one per navigation destination, mapped through
`SECTION_MARKS` and rendered both in the shell and in that section's empty state:

| Section | Mark | Reads as | Empty-state title |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Today | `sun` | the day itself | — |
| Life map | `compass` | orientation, not distance | "Your map is waiting for its first memory" |
| Journal | `quill` | the written record | "No entries match" |
| Chapters | `sprig` | something that grew over a season | "No chapters yet" |
| Paths | `route` | a direction with a turn in it | "No paths yet" |
| Trips | `ridge` | ground crossed | "No trips recorded" |
| Places & people | `shell` | something found and kept | "No places yet" |
| Collections | `leaf-cluster` | gathered by hand | "No collections yet" |
| Share preview | `feather` | a light impression, not a post | — |

Five more marks (`frond`, `moon`, `wave`, `seed`, `star`) are drawn but unused —
a reserve so a future destination gets a mark in the same hand. `art.test.ts`
holds them to the same grid and stroke rules, and the design doc says plainly
that they are unused rather than pretending the family is complete.

### Cover plates — `Cover.tsx`

`buildCover(kind, id, name)` hashes `kind:id:name` into a motif and a wash pair,
so a record's plate is stable across reloads and shifts visibly when renamed. Six
motifs (contour, strata, hachure, orbit, ripple, grid-cut) on a 120×84 plate.

### DayPlate — `Mark.tsx`

A 56×36 plate for the Today masthead: a sun behind a sea horizon, deliberately
**broken** where the disc crosses it, three water lines and two reeds in the right
margin. Two earlier attempts failed visibly and were redrawn:

1. a centred disc over a continuous horizon read as a **face**;
2. at 40×40 on a phone the reeds and water lines collapsed into noise, so the
   plate is now hidden below 560px and the date carries the masthead alone.

## 3. Tokens and both themes

Obsidian and warm paper, one amethyst accent for action and current position, no
pure black, no glass, no shadow-heavy surfaces, no gradient on an interactive
surface.

**Both themes were designed, not inverted.** `--map-canvas` is `#f6f3ec` on warm
paper and `#0d0e11` in the dark; `--art-ink` is `#8a8479` / `#b9b4a9`; the map
`--map-edge` was lifted to `#6a655c` / `#8d8f98` so the spine stops disappearing.
`npm run contrast` covers 106 pairings across both themes and both map palettes.

The single most important lesson of the stage: **a dark-only review cannot sign
off a two-theme design.** See §6.1.

## 4. Screen by screen

**Shell.** Three labelled groups — Archive (the loop: Today, Life map, Journal,
Chapters, Paths), Gather (Trips, Places & people, Collections, Share preview),
More (Projects, Timeline, Search, Roadmap, Settings). Every destination is
preserved, capture is a persistent action in the rail and the centre of the tab
bar, and all of it is ordinary links and buttons. Phones get five tabs plus
capture, and "More" is a real dialog rather than a menu that hides items
off-screen. The navigation model moved to `nav.ts` so it can be asserted.

**Today.** Time-of-day eyebrow (`Morning` / `Afternoon` / `Evening` /
`Late. Worth writing down.`), the **date as the `h1`** (`4 Nov 2026`), the day
plate as a hairline-framed printed plate, the composer as a borderless writing
surface with one hint (`Ctrl or ⌘ + Enter to save`), then today's records, then
recent ones. A dated masthead is what a printed diary prints; "Good afternoon" is
the software being cheerful at you, and it is wrong most of the time.
**The keyboard hint is hidden on touch devices**, where a shortcut is not
information a thumb can act on.

**Life map.** Shape legend (2-column glyph + name, then a one-line note that
links are dashed and that position is time, not importance) replacing a paragraph
of prose that explained the same thing abstractly. Both line types are
distinguished by **dash pattern and weight**, not colour alone, so they survive
greyscale and colour-vision deficiency. Node kinds and the detail panel's rows
use the map's own `LegendGlyph` rather than the generic icon set — at 12px the
outlined set read as a speck beside a name. Labels are placed by a resolver that
now reserves the space its `undated` / `date not exact` annotations need, keeps
3px of clearance, and elides rather than overflow (§6.6).

**Journal and entry.** Six entries visible without scrolling at 1440×950. The
entry page leads with the writing: `<h1>`, one quiet meta line, then the prose at
`--measure` — **measured 649px and 77 characters per line**, down from 1062px and
121. Below it, `Filed in <chapter>` is a real link, and `Show on the Life map`
opens `/map?focus=<id>` with the record already selected and the query string
cleaned up (verified end-to-end by `flows.mjs`).

**Chapters · Paths · Collections · Trips.** A shelf of printed plates with the
record's own name underneath — never a plate without a name. Empty states are
**one compact row** carrying the section's own mark and one sentence, and the
create action lives in the page header only. There is exactly one "New chapter"
button on an empty page, not two.

**Timeline.** The leading kind glyph and the repeated `private` badge are gone:
at 15px the outlined set smudged before the title, and the meta line already
names the kind in words. Undated records sit in their own **"Undated"** group
rather than under the day they were written (§6.7).

**Share preview.** One privacy statement instead of three. The heading eyebrow
says "A preview, not a post"; the banner says sharing is not implemented and
nothing can leave the device; the private-records toggle now reads "Only affects
this preview; still sends nothing."

**Settings.** AI card removed. Capture default mode relabelled (§7).

## 5. Copy sweep

Removed or rewritten, by category:

- **Repeated privacy statements.** Today's composer hint, the entry editor's
  private toggle, Settings' storage line, and the share page each explained the
  whole architecture. Privacy is now stated where a user would ask the question:
  first-run panel, private toggle, Settings, share preview — once each.
- **Filler.** "Add details any time", "Everything stays on this device",
  "Leaving this empty is a perfectly good choice".
- **Error strings that explain the device.** "It may have been deleted." not
  "…deleted on this device." Nothing else could have happened to it.
- **Scope creep in an empty state.** The people empty state explained a share
  model that does not exist; it now says a first name is enough.
- **The composer teaches its own shortcut** instead of repeating where text goes.

Kept deliberately: the first-run "Nothing here yet" panel (it is short, and it is
the only place a brand-new user learns where writing goes) and every destructive
confirmation.

## 6. Defects found, and how each was caught

### 6.1 The light-mode map was a black disc

`.lifemap__ring`, `.lifemap__shape-ring`, `.lifemap__shape-detail` and
`.lifemap__uncertain` were **used in the JSX and never styled**, and the four bare
`<circle>` glyphs (experience, path, person, trip) also named no fill class. SVG
defaults to `fill: black`:

- **light mode:** three concentric black circles covered the warm canvas — the
  entire map read as a solid black disc on paper;
- **dark mode:** every experience node was an invisible hollow ring.

Caught by looking at the light-mode screenshot, not by any test. Fixed by styling
every class, giving each node kind its `--map-fill-*`/`--map-ink-*` tokens, and
giving each glyph's detail marks its own kind's ink. Guarded by
`mapStyles.test.ts` (every `lifemap__*` class draws and has a rule) — verified by
reverting the fix and watching it fail.

### 6.2 Cover plates were cropped by a quarter to a half

Plates were drawn at 1.43:1 and rendered in boxes of 1.9–3.1:1 with
`preserveAspectRatio="slice"`, so the browser silently cropped every plate. A lone
collection looked like an enormous abstract tangle rather than a printed cover.

The first fix — `meet` — was worse: the wash letterboxed into a bare strip down
one side, because a plate is a **filled rectangle**, not a floating drawing.
The real fix is structural: the frame and the SVG both declare 120:84, and the
component takes no height prop, so `slice` has nothing to crop. Guarded twice —
`art.test.ts` for the markup contract, and `flows.mjs` measures the actual box in
a real browser (ratio within 0.02, wash within 1.5px).

### 6.3 Two different destinations drew the same compass

The Life map's mark was a compass and Roadmap's sidebar icon was also a compass —
two glyphs reading as the same place. Roadmap is a plan, so it is a flag now.
Guarded by a test that reads the real navigation data and refuses a glyph already
claimed by another destination. The first version of that test compared path
strings and passed on the bug; it was rewritten around the invariant it can
actually prove, then confirmed to fail on the original collision.

### 6.4 Two chapter plates were near-identical

Both drew `contour`, which rendered perfectly nested closed loops around one
centre — a ball of yarn, not a landform. Contours now drift downhill and
alternate rings run open off the plate. `art.test.ts` asserts records of one kind
get visibly different plates and that sixty draws spread over the available
motif/wash combinations.

### 6.5 Ripple rings ran past the plate

Found by the first art test: rings were clipped by the viewBox and read as a
render fault. Clamped with `maxRw` to the origin's distance to each edge.

### 6.6 The map's label geometry was measured with the wrong numbers

An earlier pass measured label boxes at 1440×950 and at 390×844, found zero
overlaps, and concluded there was no problem. Both measurements were true and
both were insufficient: the faults live at the widths in between.

Sweeping seventeen viewport widths from 390 to 1920 found three separate defects,
each of which the two chosen widths could not have shown:

1. **`labelWidth` under-measured by up to 30%.** The constant was `5.6` px per
   character; real rendered labels run `6.3`–`7.3`. The resolver therefore
   believed labels had cleared each other when they had not.
2. **The character cap was used as a pixel cap.** `labelWidth` returned
   `min(length × 5.6, cap)`, so every label longer than the budget measured as
   *exactly* the cap, however many characters it actually drew. A 31-character
   label was measured at 160px and drawn at 213px — 9px outside the canvas at
   900px wide. This is why one specific label clipped at 820 and 900 and nowhere
   else.
3. **The label placer never reserved the annotation.** The small `undated` /
   `date not exact` line sits one line away from its label, but collisions were
   only ever compared label-to-label. A label could therefore be laid straight
   across it.

All three were fixed at the source: the constants are the measured ones, `cap`
is converted to a character budget and multiplied back out, `noteWidth` and
`noteBox` give the annotation reserved space, placement requires 3px of
clearance rather than mere non-intersection, and a final elide guarantees a
label narrower than the canvas can never overflow it. `audit.mjs` now records
`clipped=` and `collisions=` for `/map` on every size, so the failures that were
specific to 820px and 1400px are covered by the standing run.

---

### 6.7 The interface printed dates the record does not claim

`occurred_precision` has six values, and an earlier copy sweep wrote
`precision !== 'exact' ? ' · approximate' : ''` into three files. That is wrong
twice over: a `day`-precision date is exact to the day, and an `unknown` date is
the opposite of a guess. Nearly every row ended up carrying the word, so it
meant nothing.

Replacing it exposed something worse underneath. Choosing "I don't know when
this happened" stores `occurred_at` as the time of writing, so lists have an
order. Every screen that printed that instant printed a date the record
explicitly does not have:

| Surface | What it read |
| --- | --- |
| Today, Journal, Chapters, Paths, Trips, Collections | `26 Sep 2026 · date unknown` |
| Entry detail | `26 September 2026 · date unknown` |
| Timeline | filed under a **"26 Sep"** day header |
| Life map detail | `2026-09-26 · approximate`, and the description leaked the raw enum: `date is approximate (unknown)` |
| Share preview | untouched raw ISO: `2026-09-26` |

Fixed by making the rule single-sourced rather than per-page: `WhenItHappened`
and `WhenExactly` in `ui/format.tsx` replace the timestamp outright when the
precision is `unknown`, and `precisionNote` now returns `null` for it so the two
cannot double up. The timeline gives undated records their own **"Undated"**
group instead of borrowing the writing date's header, and `LifeNode` gained an
`undated` flag distinct from `uncertain` — a week-only date is approximate, a
missing date is not. Found by reading a screenshot, not by a test; now covered
by `when.test.tsx` and a source scan that fails if any page slices
`occurred_at` to ten characters again.

### 6.8 The map's `undated` annotation was at 1.4:1 contrast

`--map-uncertain` was `rgba(240, 238, 233, 0.17)`, which composites to roughly
1.4:1 against the canvas — a ghost. It carried text, so it needed text contrast.
Set to the faint-text ink in both themes and added to `check-contrast.mjs`, which
had never covered the token.

---

### 6.9 Two more from the light-mode pass

**Search was the last page still using the old empty state.** Two hundred and
eighty pixels of dashed panel, a generic `search` icon, and three sentences
restating that search searches — while every other section had moved to one
compact row carrying its own drawn mark. It also had no mark of its own in the
navigation, so the first fix picked `compass` — the Life map's mark, repeating
§6.3 exactly. It is `star` now, with a comment saying why, because the next
person to reach for a mark here will make the same mistake.

**Every person was labelled `private`, and it was hardcoded.** `Person` carries a
real `visibility` field that an imported archive can populate with something
else, so the badge could contradict the record; and the sentence directly under
the list already says people never appear in a share preview, so it was also
redundant. The row now prints the stored value, and only when it is `private`.

## 7. AI removal (a behaviour change, not an interface change)

AI is out of scope for this release, so all AI-facing UI is gone: no chatbot
screen, no AI summary, no suggestion controls, no provider setting. Capture
**always** runs the local, on-device, rule-based analyzer; `captureFlow.test.ts`
asserts the fetch spy is never called during a save.

Deliberately kept, and still unit-tested, so a later release can re-attach
optional AI without a data migration: `provider.ts`, the strict remote-response
validation, and the `ai` field across `types.ts` / `dexieStore.ts` /
`src/state/aiSession`. Nothing in the UI reaches them.

One honesty fix: the stored capture mode `autonomous` was labelled **Silent** in
the UI. The engine is a parser on this device; "Autonomous" implied an agent
making decisions on the user's behalf. **The stored value is unchanged**, so no
migration and no behaviour change — it still files without asking and stays
correctable.

## 8. What still works (verified, not assumed)

`flows.mjs`, **22/22** in a real Chromium against the built app:

capture saves and appears immediately · persists across reload (IndexedDB) ·
opens the detail route · edit persists · search finds a record · search empty
state · map nodes are focusable buttons · pointer selection · **keyboard**
selection · outline view offers a text route · private records excluded from the
share preview by default · **no publish control exists** · reduced motion zeroes
the transition tokens · no horizontal overflow at 200 % text · every cover is
`aria-hidden` · every cover is paired with a real record name · a cover is stable
across a reload · navigation carries the drawn marks · **a cover keeps its
proportion and its wash fills the plate** · **an empty section draws its own mark
on one compact row** · **one create action per page, not two** · **a focused map
link opens with that record selected**.

The last two of those were confirmed to be real checks, not decoration: each was
run against a deliberately reverted build and watched to fail. The map-link check
opens the entry the run itself created, because a hardcoded id would pass on a
fresh profile while testing nothing.

`audit.mjs` renders all 15 routes at 1440×950 and 390×844, in both themes, with
no horizontal overflow — 60 route renders — and records `clipped=` /
`collisions=` for every `/map` render.

**Additional geometry sweep**, run separately because the failures were
size-specific: the Life map at 17 viewport widths from 390 to 1920, measuring
every label and annotation box against the canvas and against each other.
Final result: **0 clipped, 0 collisions at every width**.

## 9. Remaining gaps (honest)

1. **Not release-ready on the strength of this evidence.** Everything above was
   verified in headless Chromium inside a Linux sandbox whose only UI font is
   Inter. Real devices (Android, iOS, macOS with SF, Windows with Segoe) have not
   been looked at, and font metrics change every measure on this page.
2. **No human has read this build on a phone.** Mobile was verified by 390px
   screenshots and a measurement harness. Thumb reach, real touch targets under a
   finger, and Safari's viewport behaviour are unverified.
3. **Not tested with a screen reader.** The accessibility work is structural —
   landmarks, labels, focus order, `aria-hidden` on decorative art, duplicate
   announcement avoidance — and it is verified by markup assertions. No NVDA,
   VoiceOver or TalkBack session was run.
4. **Light mode had less review than dark for most of the stage**, and that is
   precisely how §6.1 survived as long as it did. A final pass did go screen by
   screen through light mode; two more defects came out of it (§6.9), which is
   evidence that the gap was real rather than theoretical. Both themes are now
   reviewed to the same depth, but neither has been reviewed by another person.
5. **Five marks are drawn but unused** (`frond`, `moon`, `wave`, `seed`, `star`).
   They are consistent and tested, but they are reserve, not shipped vocabulary.
6. **The tab bar's five destinations are a judgement call.** Trips, Places,
   Collections and Share preview live behind "More" on a phone. They are reachable
   and the sheet is a real dialog, but this is a compromise, not a solved problem.
7. **`autonomous` → "Silent" is a label change only.** Anyone who knew the old
   label will not find it in Settings, and nothing in the interface explains the
   rename.
8. **The artwork is evaluated as craft, not as art direction by a designer.** It
   is consistent, deterministic, token-driven and testable; whether it is
   *good* is a judgement this report cannot make for you.
